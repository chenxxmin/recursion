# Response to Reviewer 1

Thank you for your helpful comments.

**Regarding novelty and mechanistic insight.** **Our contribution is a quantitative characterization of how task structure affects the learning of sequential linear recurrences.** By varying recurrence order, rule count, and masking, we identify task-dependent benefits of depth, width, and curriculum training. The known algebraic structure also lets us identify candidate prediction formulas and relate shortcut availability to learning across positions. Together, these results explain which interventions help particular tasks and which strategies are consistent with model predictions.

**Regarding experimental design: Inter- versus intra-trajectory learning.** **These settings represent two complementary forms of multi-task sequence learning.** Inter abstracts adaptation to an implicit, persistent domain or style. A model continuing an unlabelled technical passage must infer its linguistic conventions from context and maintain them. Intra abstracts successive explicit instructions, such as summarizing and then translating. The instruction identifies the current operation, but the model must still execute it using the accumulated context.

We have revised the interpretation to present these as complementary settings and withdraw the direct difficulty comparison. Intra tags specify the independently sampled rule at each step; hiding that choice can leave the same observed prefix compatible with different next values. We add A′ and B to examine sequence format and explicit rule information more closely:

| Condition | Rule within a sequence | Rule information | 2 rules | 3 rules | 10 rules | 20 rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Fixed | No tag | 2/2 | 2/4* | — | — |
| A′ | Fixed | Uninformative NULL tokens | 2/2 | Pending* | — | — |
| B | Fixed | Repeated rule tag | 2/2 | — | 2/2 | 1/2† |
| C | May change | Current rule tag | 2/2 | — | 2/2 | 0/1† |

Entries count available seeds reaching 99% test accuracy; — means no result reported here. *Three-rule A/A′ use width 2048; other entries use width 1024, all with two layers. †Additional seeds are pending; unsuccessful recorded runs ended before the maximum budget.

A and A′ reach comparable final accuracy in the two-rule baseline; tagged B and C both learn ten rules. These are capability observations under their respective protocols. Differences in rule pools, supervision, and training settings preclude attributing the A′/B contrast solely to tags.

**Regarding shortcuts and strategy identification.** **A delayed shortcut cannot cover all supervised positions, and our analysis does not prescribe a uniquely preferred formula.** A shortcut with maximum lag $k'$ is unavailable for the first $k'-k$ predictions, which remain supervised (Section 3.3; Appendix C.1, Eq. 7). Indeed, Appendix D.3 reports models consistent with using the original recurrence at early positions and a sparse shortcut later.

**Section 5.3 tests whether high prediction accuracy is accompanied by sensitivity to redundant history.** We preserve sufficient local information while perturbing earlier context, then test whether the model still follows the current rule after global trajectory consistency is broken (Appendix G.3). Strategies that agree on valid trajectories can respond differently to this intervention. Distinguishing them therefore characterizes robustness to the tested context perturbations, without assuming that either strategy is universally preferable.

**Regarding presentation.** **We have prepared a methods overview and a unified capability roadmap for the main text.** The overview explains formula fitting on randomized inputs, interventions testing local rule agreement, and probes measuring rule decodability. The roadmap consolidates the depth/width findings and connects Sections 3–5, with detailed derivations retained in the appendix. Both additions appear in the accompanying [revision text](manuscript_revision_text.md).
